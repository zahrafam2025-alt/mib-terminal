What the Headlines Missed

The index grabbed the headlines, but underneath it fewer and fewer stocks were actually rising — just 34% by Friday, down from 56% and as low as 31% mid-week. A rally that thins out like that is easier to knock over. The gains kept coming from the same narrow group — names like chipmakers (SOXX), chipmakers (SMH) and biotech (XBI) — while MRVL, Micron and XES were left behind.

One Story, Properly Told

One story dominated the week and kept shifting under people's feet: it began as Strait of Hormuz shipping dispute ongoing; Iran-Oman negotiation framework emerging but unresolved, keeping energy supply premium in play and by Friday had become US-Iran Strait of Hormuz. It topped the week because it was the one force reshaping which corners of the market investors wanted to own.

In Plain English

  • EPS — earnings per share — a company's profit divided by its share count; the number Wall Street forecasts and compares against.
  • cons. — 'consensus' — the average analyst forecast. Markets move on the RESULT vs this expectation, not the result alone.
  • YoY — year-over-year — compared with the same period last year.
  • FOMC — the US Federal Reserve committee that sets interest rates — its decisions move nearly every market.
  • risk-on — money comfortable buying risky assets like stocks and crypto; safe havens get sold.
  • VIX — the market's 'fear gauge'. Higher = traders expect bigger swings; ~20 is the long-run average.
  • hawkish — leaning toward higher rates / tighter policy — generally a headwind for stocks.
  • CPI — the headline inflation reading (consumer price index) — moves rate expectations and therefore stocks and bonds.
